Yasaman Kargar

Ph.D. Candidate in Materials Science & Engineering, Washington University in St. Louis

St. Louis, MO

I am a fourth-year Ph.D. candidate in the Institute of Materials Science and Engineering (IMSE) at Washington University in St. Louis, advised by Prof. Nathaniel Huebsch.

My research focuses on how mechanical cues influence heart development and disease progression. I build human induced pluripotent stem cell–derived cardiomyocyte (hiPS-CM) models of inherited cardiomyopathy, with a focus on desmin-related cardiomyopathy (DRM), which affects about 1 in 2,000 individuals, and hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM), which affects 1 in 500 to 1 in 200 individuals. My work combines engineered micro-heart tissues (µHTs), CRISPR-edited disease lines, and tunable magnetorheological elastomer (MRE) substrates to study how mechanical load shapes the onset and progression of cardiac disease.

My broader interests include cardiac tissue engineering, mechanobiology, cellular and molecular biology, and stem cell engineering.

Before my Ph.D., I received my B.Sc. in Materials Science and Engineering from Sharif University of Technology. I also received my M.Sc. in Materials Science and Engineering from WashU in 2024.

Outside of research, I enjoy running, reading, traveling, playing tennis, and watching films.
